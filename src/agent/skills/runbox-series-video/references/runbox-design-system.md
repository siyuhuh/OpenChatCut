# Runbox Design System

## Visual premise

Runbox turns scattered local project work into one controllable surface. The recurring visual idea is therefore routing and convergence, not generic futuristic technology.

## Palette

- Background: `#111512`
- Raised surface: `#1C251E`
- Primary text: `#F4FBF5`
- Secondary text: `#B4C3B7`
- Accent: `#9BEA78`
- Dim accent: `#4F7A40`
- Grid and route lines: `#304237`

Use the accent for the active path, selected state, conclusion, or one key word. Large areas of bright green weaken the hierarchy.

## Typography

- English display: `Inter Tight`
- Chinese and body copy: `Noto Sans SC`
- Commands, paths, Git state, and metadata: `Space Mono`

Use short display phrases. Long Chinese explanations belong in captions or narration, not inside a Motion Graphic.

## Geometry

- Prefer lines, rails, nodes, brackets, windows, tabs, and file-path structures.
- Keep corners square or lightly rounded; avoid pill-heavy layouts.
- Use a consistent thin line weight and one heavier active route.
- Prefer an asymmetric composition with one clear destination.

## Motion language

- Reveal major groups through clipping or directional movement.
- Grow routes from source to destination.
- Stagger related items by 6-10 frames.
- Use a firm spring settle with damping around 28 and stiffness around 90.
- Avoid bounce, decorative glow, glass surfaces, and continuous ambient motion.
- Hold the readable state, then exit in 12-18 frames.

## Series continuity

Keep the palette, type roles, line weights, and timing rhythm stable across episodes. Vary the explanatory mechanism: a route map, checkpoint chain, command flow, or interface crop should reflect the episode's actual idea.

## Representative first graphic

For the Runbox introduction, use `Convergence Map`: three labeled work streams enter from different directions, their routes resolve into one Runbox destination, and the title appears only after the paths settle. This turns the spoken problem—parallel projects becoming chaotic—into a visible before/after relationship.
